# Playtest feedback — 18 September 2026 (S20 Ultra)

Seven items from a playtest of the movement build. Each has what was wrong, what changed, and how
it was checked. "Probe" means `MovementProbe` running the real game headless in the Editor (see
the end of this page).

| # | Item | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ball rolls along a part's edge, in the air | Fixed, measured |
| 2 | One-tap turns the wrong way after running near an edge | Fixed, measured |
| 3 | Revived ball falls straight away | Fixed, measured |
| 4 | Path animation | Replaced, not yet seen on a device |
| 5 | Next part's edges drawn over the part under the ball | Fix tried and **reverted**: it caused other visual problems |
| 6 | Bolt trail starts narrow, bulges, narrows | Fixed (all four trails), not yet seen on a device |
| 7 | More path patterns | Five added, ready to try |

---

## 1. The ball rode along an edge, in the air

**Cause (confirmed, and it came from the movement revamp):** `PlayerMovement.findRestHeight` finds
the ground with a sphere cast of the ball's full radius. A sphere also touches a part's *edge*
while the ball hangs over it, and surfaces up to 70° counted as ground — so the ball stayed
"on the track" until its centre was about 0.23 past the edge (worked out from the 70° limit and
the 0.25 radius). The old physics would have rolled it off.

The "path showing through the ball" in that state is most likely the same thing: hanging on the
corner, the ball sits lower than the surface, so the part really is in front of some of it. Not
reproduced separately.

**Fix:** there must also be track under the ball's centre (`supportUnderCentre`, a 0.04 sphere
cast — small, but it cannot slip between two parts at a seam).

**Measured** (`-probeEdge`, pushing the ball outwards 0.01 per step): it now leaves the track with
its centre **0.048** past the edge. Parts are 2.5 wide.

## 2. One-tap chose the wrong direction

**Cause (confirmed):** a one-tap turn went the way stored in `PathMaker.nextDirection`, which each
part's Destroyer trigger sets when the ball enters it. The triggers are 2×2 on 2.5-wide parts —
smaller on purpose, so two turn parts side by side do not both fire — so a ball running near the
edge could miss one, and the tap used an old direction, or none.

**Fix:** when the ball is on a turn part (`LandLeft` / `LandRight`), that part decides. The part
comes from the same probe as item 1, so it is always the part under the ball's centre. A part
that has already turned the ball ignores more taps. A tap anywhere else behaves as before —
it turns towards the next turn — so tapping too early or too late still ends the run.

Auto-pilot and bolt turns already worked this way (they turn by the part's own tag at its centre).

**Measured** (`-probeOneTap`: auto-pilot off, a tap within ±0.2 of each turn part's centre, and a
second tap one step later): 150 turns at normal speed and 150 at top speed with every pattern
unlocked, no falls. In one 200-turn run, 265 of 401 taps were correctly ignored as "already turned
here" — the second taps, and taps just after a bolt had turned the ball.

## 3. The revived ball fell again

**Cause (confirmed):** after a revive the ball set off one second after the new path started
building, at the speed it had when it fell. The path is 15 parts, spawned 0.15 s apart, each taking
0.8 s to drop, so the last landed 2.9 s after the start. In theory a ball at top speed stayed about
0.1 s behind the parts. That is less than the frame-rate rounding in `WaitForSeconds`. The movement
revamp removed the 24% speed loss that used to add slack.

**Fix:** the revived ball waits in place until the first 15 parts have landed
(`releaseWhenPathHasLanded`, `PathMaker.firstPartsHaveLanded`). The revive path is also built
faster (0.08 s apart) and drops faster (item 4), so the wait is short.

**Measured** (`-probeRevive`: knock the ball off with a chance active):

| | back on the track after the fall | falls in the next 15 turns |
|---|---|---|
| top speed | 4.4 s (was 5.5 s) | 0 |
| normal speed | 3.8 s (was 5.2 s) | 0 |

## 4. Path animation

**Before:** each part fell a fixed 0.25 per physics step from 10 units up — a constant speed that
stopped dead, 0.8 s long, and moved in physics-step jumps on 90/120 Hz screens.

**Now** (`MoveDown`): it falls on game time, moving every frame, fast at first and slowing into
place, with a 1% settle below its spot, in 0.5 s.

Other styles worth trying if this one is not it: rising up from below (fits the name), popping
in with a slight scale bounce, tiles flipping into place, or the drop with a small squash on
landing.

## 5. The next part's edges drawn over the part under the ball

**Cause (confirmed):** the path materials (`WhiteShine.mat` and the like) are URP Lit set to
*Transparent*: no depth write, alpha blending. `PathMaker.updatePartsRenderQueue` keeps those
settings on the part under the ball and the one before it, and switches the parts ahead to
`LitFadeWhenClose`, which does write depth. The parts were queued nearest-first. That meant the
part ahead was drawn after a nearer part that had written no depth, and it came out on top
wherever the two overlapped on screen.

The near parts not writing depth is what lets a planet's moons (queue 2400, `SolarSystem`) show
through the track, so that stays.

**Tried, then reverted:** queuing the parts furthest-first (commit b5c49df) cured the overlap, but
on the S20 it caused several other visual problems, so the original order is back (4a3a40b). The
overlap is minor and stays for now. A proper fix would show the moons through the track some
other way, so every part can write depth.

## 6. Trails

**Cause (confirmed):** all four trail width curves started at 17–25% of their width and only
reached it 2.5–4% of the way along. At top speed that stretch is longer than the ball.

**Fix:** each curve starts at its peak and tapers from there; the rest of the curve is unchanged.
Normal, bolt, double points and chance trails.

Ideas, not done yet:

- **Rounded corners at turns.** The trails have no corner vertices, so every turn is a sharp mitre.
- **Lying flat on the track** like a light trail, instead of turning to face the camera.
- **The same length at any speed.** The trail has a fixed lifetime, so at top speed it is 2.3× as
  long as at the start.
- **A comet for the bolt:** as wide as the ball at the head, with a few sparks.
- **A soft-edged glow texture** across the width.

## 7. Path patterns

The pattern ladder moved from `ScoreManager` into `PathMaker.unlockPatternsUpTo`, unchanged, and
five new set pieces were added. R and L are turn parts, S a flat straight part.

| Score over | Existing | New |
|---|---|---|
| — | R, L | |
| 110 | R-S-R, L-S-L, R-S-L, L-S-R | **flat run** S-S-R, S-S-L · **short climbs**: three legs in a row with no ramp between curve up and curve out |
| 200 | R-S-R-S-R, L-S-L-S-L, R-S-R-S-L, L-S-L-S-R | **wide sidestep** R-S-S-L, L-S-S-R · **spiral**: the next four turns all the same way, winding up around itself |
| 300 | drops the 110 four; R-R, L-L, R-L, L-R | |
| 400 | R-R-S-R, L-L-S-L | |
| 500 | R-R-L, L-L-R, R-L-R, L-R-L | **long slalom** R-L-R-L, L-R-L-R |

Spirals and short climbs each start on one pick in eight, and never at the same time (the loops
would stack only 12 units apart).

**Trying them without playing to 500:** in a development build, a file called `try-patterns` in
the game's files folder unlocks everything from the first turn:

```bash
adb shell touch /sdcard/Android/data/com.AbuSada.RisingWay/files/try-patterns
```

Delete it to go back to the normal ladder. All of the probe runs above were made with everything
unlocked.

Ideas that need new track parts: a gap to jump, a descending ramp, a narrow bridge, parts that
move sideways, parts that crumble shortly after the ball passes, a fork where one branch is a
dead end, and a speed pad.

**Worth knowing:** back-to-back turns (R-L and friends) are now 0.15 s apart at top speed, against
0.2 s before the movement revamp, because the ball now reaches the speed it is asked for (see
`movement-revamp.md`). If the late game feels too tight, lower `TOP_PLAYER_SPEED` rather than
changing the patterns.

## Follow-ups, 19 September

**The revive must not pause the game.** Item 3's fix made the ball wait for the path, which fixed
the falls but felt like a pause. The revive path is now built so it is already down when the ball
arrives. Its parts are placed 0.03 s apart on a clock, not per frame, so a slow phone cannot
stretch it, and each drops in 0.3 s. All 15 are down 0.72 s in, and the ball takes 1 s to fly back.
The wait stays as a safety net and logs "[Revive] the ball waited ..." in development builds.
Probe: waited **0.00 s** at both speeds; back on the track 3.4 s after the fall at top speed and
3.3 s at normal speed (5.5 s and 5.2 s before this round). No falls in the next 15 turns.

**A bolt puts the ball back on the path.** A player who turned off the path to reach a bolt used
to carry on sideways at bolt speed and fall. Picking up a bolt now turns the ball the way the path
goes at that part and puts it on the part's centre line (`PlayerMovement.rejoinPathAt`). Arriving
the normal way on a turn part, it still turns on the centre as before. Probe
(`-probeBoltRejoin`, steering off the path for 4 steps and then doing what the pickup does):
24 times at normal speed and 24 at top speed, no falls.

## The probe runs

```bash
"C:\Program Files\Unity\Hub\Editor\6000.5.7f1\Editor\Unity.exe" -batchmode -projectPath C:\Work\RisingWay -executeMethod MovementProbeRunner.Run -movementProbe -probeTurns 150 -probeAllPatterns -logFile probe.log
```

| Flag | What it does |
|---|---|
| `-probeFast` | holds top speed and the bolt time scale |
| `-probeEdge` | pushes the ball sideways until it leaves the track; reports how far past the edge |
| `-probeRevive` | knocks the ball off with a chance active; checks the revive and the next 15 turns |
| `-probeOneTap` | auto-pilot off; taps like a player, ±0.2 around each turn part's centre, plus a second tap |
| `-probeAllPatterns` | every pattern from the first turn |
| `-probeBoltRejoin` | steers the ball off the path, then does what picking up a bolt does |
| `-probeVerbose` | logs every tap, turn and lost-ground event |
